## 2. Problem

**The listing ended. The photos didn't necessarily disappear.**

When a home sells or a rental is taken down, the listing usually changes status. The photos, floor plan, and virtual tour can stay attached to the property page, where anyone who searches the address can scroll through them.

The same photos are often copied to several property websites, the original brokerage's site, and image search results. Each site has its own rules. Getting photos taken down on one doesn't necessarily take them down anywhere else.

Not every home is affected. The way to know is to look, carefully, in all the places copies tend to end up.

What this doesn't cover:
- Erasing the address, public records, sale history, or tax data
- Exterior or satellite images, and map services other than the optional Street View request
- Records inside the MLS. Hiding a listing publicly is different from deleting it there
- Every copy on the internet, people-search and data-broker sites, or monitoring after day 60

How we count a result: Only what we check ourselves. A request we sent, or a page that won't load, isn't proof. A result means we independently confirmed the content is removed or no longer shown publicly, and we kept dated evidence. Websites decide how and when they respond, and the original listing source can affect what they'll hide.

A sample final report (labeled "Illustrative example", fictional rows) shows destination, what was found at the start, the request, the check date, and status, followed by an "Unresolved" line. Status definitions:
- **Request submitted:** We asked. Nothing is confirmed yet. A request on its own isn't counted as a result.
- **Verified suppressed / Verified removed:** We checked independently, and the content no longer shows publicly. Some sites hide content rather than delete it. Removed means it's gone from that page.
- **Still visible:** The content is still public. We say so, along with what we tried and what happens next.
